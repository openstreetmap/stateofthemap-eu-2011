Emergency preparedness powered by OSM: finding the safest evacuation routes in case of earthquake in Thessaloniki

Matina Halkia, Joint Research Centre, European Commission

Details about the talk:
Social media technologies have been identified to have played a role in
crises. Traits such as responsiveness, flexibility, self-organization,
improvisation, resilience, agility seem pertinent for forward-looking risk and crisis management. Social media are attributed with social
convergence; they facilitate people’s participation in emergency
management; they support collective intelligence in a crisis. Crisis
management begs for something more than raw information. “Actionable knowledge” is required: usable, useful and immediately relevant information. Social media in crisis contexts promote the value of collective intelligence that can nourish actionable knowledge; they can effectively fertilize formal crisis information management. Applied
technology development based on these principles begs for expanding available collaborative distributed platforms such OSM with appropriate modules, which can be adapted and exploited real-time and on the fly according to the crisis context. This work describes such a module and an interface powered by OSM, which responds to a crisis situation by locating the most effective evacuation route in a city in the context of a severe earthquake. We present the principles, guidelines, method, and a prototype based on a case study for the city of Thessaloniki, Greece. Based on the guidelines developed, specific to the type of disaster, we identify destination locations (evacuation sites), use building footprint information extracted from existing data, identify possible routes, and perform a weighted analysis of the best evacuation route, using distance and taking into account topological characteristics and safety criteria. The data management, processing and analysis is carried out on OSM data using open-source software. Finally, we present this information in order to provide “actionable knowledge”: usable, useful and immediately relevant.

Details about the speaker:
Matina Halkia is an architect engineer and human computer interaction expert. Her core research interests lie in augmented reality/ICT applications in physical/architectural space. She is currently working on emergency response map validation and urban analysis for remote sensing applications in human security contexts. She has studied Architecture and Urban Studies at UCL, History of Art and Architecture at Tufts University under a Fulbright Scholarship, and Media Arts and Sciences at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. She has worked as an interaction designer in Boston (1997-1998), and as principal investigator at Starlab Laboratories, a technology think-tank, in Brussels (1998-2001). Since 2001 she works for the Joint Research Centre of the European Commission, currently at the Global Security and Crisis Management Unit, as a scientific officer.
